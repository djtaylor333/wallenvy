How Much Does Direct-to-Wall Printing Cost in Australia?

Posted October 24 2026


If you've been looking into direct-to-wall printing, you've probably noticed that getting a clear idea of the cost isn't always straightforward.

Some providers advertise a price per square metre, while others quote each project individually. And when you start looking at the different types of walls, artwork and printing requirements, it's easy to see why one price doesn't fit every job.

So, how much does direct-to-wall printing actually cost?

The honest answer is that it depends on the wall, the artwork and what's involved in producing the finished result.

At Wall Envy, we take a project-by-project approach. We're focused on delivering a professional result, not being the cheapest operator in the market.

We're a specialist direct-to-wall printing business based on the Central Coast, servicing Newcastle, Lake Macquarie and the wider Hunter region. We understand the technology, the practical limitations of the equipment and what needs to happen before a wall is ready to print.

If you're investing in a feature wall, business logo or large-scale mural, we believe those things matter.

What affects the cost of direct-to-wall printing?



There are several factors that influence the final price. Understanding them helps explain why two walls of the same size can have quite different quotes.

You may see wall printing described online as an seamless within the printable area

1. The size of the print



The overall print area is an important starting point.

A small company logo behind a reception desk is a different job from a large mural covering an entire feature wall.

However, the price doesn't always increase in direct proportion to the square metres being printed. Every project involves some fixed work, including communication, planning, equipment transport and setup.

For smaller jobs, these costs can represent a significant part of the total. Larger projects may allow those costs to be spread across a greater print area.

That's why we look at the complete job rather than relying on square metres alone.

2. The condition and type of wall



Not every wall is ready for printing.

A clean, well-maintained painted wall may need very little preparation. An older brick wall, heavily textured surface or wall with damaged paint may require additional work or assessment.

We consider factors such as:
- The type of substrate and existing finish.
- Surface texture and variations in height.
- Dust, grease, loose paint or other contaminants.
- Whether repairs, cleaning or suitable coatings are needed.
- Whether the printer can access and travel along the wall safely.

Our MK02 printer can work with a range of suitable architectural surfaces, but no responsible operator should promise that every wall is suitable without considering its actual condition.

Getting this right before printing helps avoid disappointing results and unnecessary costs.

3. Artwork and design preparation



Have you already got a high-resolution image or company logo ready to go? Great — that can simplify the process.

If the artwork needs substantial adjustment, however, additional design work may be required.

This could include resizing an image, improving its resolution, removing a background, adjusting colours or arranging the design to suit the available printable area.

For commercial projects, we also consider how the artwork will look in the actual space. A design that looks good on a computer screen doesn't always translate perfectly to a large wall.

We can provide digital mock-ups where appropriate, helping you visualise the proposed result before printing begins.

4. White ink and specialist printing effects



Wall Envy's MK02 uses UV-curable CMYK plus white ink.

White ink can be important when printing onto dark or coloured surfaces, particularly when the artwork contains bright colours or areas that need an opaque white finish.

Depending on the design, white ink may be used as part of the image or as a white underbase beneath other colours.

We also offer relief and embossed printing effects for projects where a more tactile or dimensional finish is desired.

These applications can involve additional planning and printing considerations, so the requirements of the artwork and surface will be reflected in the quote.

5. Wall height, access and machine clearances



The MK02 can print up to approximately four metres high in its standard configuration, subject to site conditions.

However, the total wall dimensions aren't necessarily the same as the printable area.

The machine requires working clearances around ceilings, floors and adjoining walls. Windows, doorways, pipes, conduits and other fixtures can also affect the layout.

For example, a wall measuring three metres high might have a smaller usable print height once the required clearances are allowed for.

We assess these details before confirming the final printable dimensions.

If you're planning a commercial fit-out or renovation, involving us early can help avoid design changes later.

You can find the machine's measurements and planning requirements in ourTechnical Specifications Guide →

6. Travel and project location



Where the job is located can also affect the cost.

Wall Envy is based on the Central Coast and focuses on serving customers across the Central Coast, Newcastle, Lake Macquarie, Maitland, Cessnock and the wider Hunter region.

Unlike Sydney-based operators travelling into the region, we're locally based and can work directly with customers, builders, designers and businesses in the area.

That local presence is valuable when a project requires a site visit, measurements, design discussions or coordination with other trades.

Any travel outside our usual service area can be discussed when preparing the quote.

7. Surface preparation and project coordination



The best results start with a suitable surface.

Depending on the wall, preparation might involve cleaning, repairing damaged areas, removing loose material or applying an appropriate coating.

Commercial jobs may also need to be coordinated around business hours, other trades, furniture, access restrictions or the installation of fixtures.

We discuss these requirements upfront so you have a clearer understanding of what's involved and what needs to be ready before printing day.

Why choose a specialist rather than simply the cheapest quote?



Direct-to-wall printing is a specialised service. The printer is only one part of producing a successful result.

Understanding artwork, substrates, machine clearances, surface preparation and the practical limitations of the equipment is just as important.

At Wall Envy, we use the MK02 and have developed our service around the technical requirements of printing directly onto real architectural surfaces.

Our approach includes assessing the wall, confirming the printable area, considering the artwork and planning the job before the printer arrives.

We also offer capabilities beyond standard flat wall graphics, including white underbase printing and relief or embossed effects.

For customers, that means having one specialist to discuss the project with, rather than having to work out the technical details themselves.

Our aim isn't to win every job by offering the lowest price. It's to deliver a result we're confident putting our name to.

Is direct-to-wall printing worth the investment?



That depends on what you're trying to achieve.

For a business, a printed feature wall can turn an ordinary reception area, office, café or retail space into something that reflects the brand and creates a stronger impression on customers.

For schools and community facilities, custom murals can transform otherwise plain spaces. For homes, direct printing offers a way to create artwork designed specifically for an existing wall.

It's worth comparing the result you want with other options, such as wallpaper, vinyl graphics, hand-painted murals or printed panels. Each has its place, and the best choice depends on the application.

Direct-to-wall printing offers a different approach: the artwork is printed directly onto a suitable surface rather than being applied as a separate wallpaper or vinyl layer.

When the design, wall and application are right, it can be a distinctive way to transform a space.
